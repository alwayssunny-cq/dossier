## 1. About / Identity

**What is Closequarters Club?**
Closequarters Club designs narrative-led travel experiences — stories you can step into.

**Ethos**
The greatest experiences of your life shouldn't be by accident.

Travel always solved *where*, not *how it feels*. Everyone has that one trip they still talk
about; at CQ every trip has it. Those moments happen by luck → We believe those moments can be
designed.

- We don't leave meaning to chance.
- We are not selling travel.
- We are selling a designed emotional outcome. Travel is just the medium.

## 2. Photography

**Anti-Stock Energy**
Avoid those oversaturated stains on society like the plague. You know, those insufferable travel
stock photos your subconscious just rolled its eyes over.

**Half-open Doors**
Look for imagery that feels like you're stealing a glimpse — active human moments caught halfway,
or partially covered behind a veil.

**Sincerity in Faces**
When people are featured, ensure they aren't posing, smiling at the camera, or acting as
"models." Needs to be like you've captured them unseen.

**Litmus**
> Does this feel like an advertisement? If yes, find another.

## 3. Type

### Tier 0 — TAN Mon Cheri
Use it like a unicorn — sparingly, oversized, and surrounded by immense negative space. Never for
full sentences or critical informational headers.
*Placement:* cover pages, single-word titles, massive, transition screens.

### Tier 1 — The Seasons
Go-to header font, and quotes, thanks to its elegant sweeping italics.
*Placement:* chapter headings and section titles, quotes.

### Tier 2 — Optima
Less flair, more grounded, great visual clarity. The body font.
*Placement:* all body copy, labels, data points, system UI, small captions beneath photography.

### Size / guardrail table

| Tier | Font | Digital size | Role / guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 0 (Title) | TAN Mon Cheri | 64px or greater | Keep it very short, add generous padding. |
| Tier 1 (Header) | The Seasons | 32px to 40px | For structural titling. |
| Tier 2 (Body) | Optima | 16px | Line-height at least 1.5 for an airy, minimal look. |

### Why it works
Seasons and Optima have chemistry. Optima is a humanist sans-serif — serious and grounded, but
timeless. Moving from the sharp, high-contrast serif of Seasons to the clean lines of Optima is
seamless; they share a classical genetic energy. When TAN Mon Cheri occasionally breaks through it
injects life and colour without reading as a mistake — which is exactly why it must stay rare.

### Rules
1. **Never stack Tier 0 and Tier 1.** Do not place a TAN Mon Cheri title directly above a Seasons
   sub-header. If Mon Cheri is on the page, the next text should skip straight to Optima body copy,
   or be separated by a physical line / large gap.
2. **Mon Cheri, no caps.** Never all-caps. Give it at least 60% empty space to breathe.
3. **Sub-headers.** Optima Bold more than does the job. Seasons is precious too, albeit not as much
   as Mon Cheri.

## 4. Voice

It's a balancing act to carry intellectual weight while revealing a romantic or imaginative nature.

### Attributes

**Concise** — We write with extreme restraint. Shorter blocks of text, no fluff, negative space
carries weight. We never over-explain, boast, or beg for attention.

**Atmospheric** — We invoke psychological and sensory responses by focusing on textures, narratives
and history of the subject — sometimes leaning into a "vintage fairy tale" romance.

**Authoritative, but quietly** — We speak as equals to our clients, not as servants. Calm,
discerning, steady. No high-pitched exclamation points, jargon, or buzzwords.

### Tone

**Smart, but never academic** (or formal, mon coeur)
- Instead of: "We design for human architecture—matching your internal rhythm to a specific geographic reality."
- Write: "Every interaction feels like visiting a friend of a friend. Be cared for by people who truly get you, even before you say hello."

**Evocative, but never dramatic**
- Instead of: "Prepare for a breath-taking, mind-blowing, once-in-a-lifetime spectacular adventure!"
- Write: "The landscape is quiet, vast, and completely indifferent to the modern world."

**Exclusive, but never elitist**
- Instead of: "An ultra-premium, VIP-only club reserved strictly for high-net-worth individuals."
- Write: "A quiet circle for those who value depth over display."

### The Banned List
*Sometimes it's all about what you omit.*

| ✗ yuck | ✓ yum |
|---|---|
| Luxury, Premium, Elite, High-end | Intentional, Rare, Restrained, Deep |
| Bespoke, Tailor-made, Custom | Designed, Crafted |
| VIP, Experiential, Bucket-list | Intimate, Solitary, Resonance, Shift |
| Wanderlust, Travel, Vacation | Journey, Geography, Reality |
| Breathtaking, Stunning, Magical | Raw, Quiet, Textured, Evocative |

### In Practice

**Marketing hook** — cinematic & intriguing.
> "Accommodations with flair and character, ranging from the exquisite to the eccentric."

**Service description** — thoughtful and specific.
> "You'd be surprised how much we uncover through our onboarding rituals, like the Reading—from
> preferences and priorities to morning routines, and deeper still: the places you've felt most at
> home, or how celebrated your life makes you feel."

**Client communications** — direct, clear, unhurried.
> Dear Puneet,
> Thank you for bringing such charm and authenticity to your Reading.
> We feel like we're a good match - and we'd like to extend our Invitation. Should you feel the
> same, you may accept it with an advance of INR 23,232.

### The Litmus Test
> Could this copy be used by Marriott Bonvoy? If yes, throw it out and rewrite. It needs to sound
> like it could only come from us and no one else.

## 6. Reference sites — how it should move

The book closes with screenshots captioned *"References of websites and sections to help you
understand how it moves: can we apply this UI UX elements."* Sites shown: HUTS, a designer-survey
report, STAGE&CREW, State of AI Design 2026 (stateofaidesign.com), a Featured Work grid, a
brutalist agency site, Jurni AI (Bonhomme), The Eight, and PRODUX.

The interaction vocabulary they share:

1. **Split-screen scroll** — one column pinned/sticky while the adjacent column scrolls past it.
2. **Horizontal rails** — cards in a draggable/scrolling row that runs past the viewport edge
   rather than wrapping into a grid.
3. **Collage over grid** — overlapping image planes at differing z-index and scale, with small
   labels tucked against their corners.
4. **Type over image** — display type crossing in front of and behind imagery in the same line.
5. **Numbered section indices** — `01 —`, `02 —`, `(05) CATEGORIES` as editorial wayfinding.
6. **Letterspaced micro-caps** — tiny tracked-out uppercase labels for metadata and nav.
7. **Hairline ornamental frames** — thin rules that box a region, with decorative corner marks
   (The Eight).
8. **Hover-revealed metadata** — tag pills and a `VIEW ↗` affordance appearing on card hover.
9. **Explicit scroll cues** — "Scroll to read" with an arrow at the fold.
10. **Flat colour stat panels** — large figures in solid blocks abutting photography.
11. **Enormous negative space around a single display word** (PRODUX) — the Tier 0 rule, rendered
    as layout.

Note: several references are loud (fluoro purple, orange, brutalist black). Adopt their **motion
and structure**, not their palette. CQ's four colours hold.
